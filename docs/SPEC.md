# ScanToAlibre specification

Status: draft, from the scoping discussion of 11 November 2026. The add-on skeleton and an Insert Test Surface command are built and tested: `DesignSurfaces.InsertFromFile` inserts our IGES B-spline patch into a part, and an extrude To Geometry in Alibre's interface stops at it. The other Alibre calls listed here are untried.

## Purpose

Turn a 3D scan (an STL mesh or a point cloud) into a real, editable Alibre Design part. The user identifies features on the scan interactively, and the add-on creates Alibre features from them. Freeform areas get fitted B-spline surfaces.

## Platform

- An Alibre Design add-on in C++/MFC, x64, targeting Alibre 27.0.1.27039. The generated 27.0.1 headers are the authority (the API docs describe v29).
- Same layout as CAMForAlibre: `addon\` (the only code that calls Alibre or MFC), `core\` (a static library with no Alibre or MFC code, millimetres, UTF-8, tested), `tests\` (doctest), `third_party\` (pinned copies with licences).
- Planned libraries: Eigen (MPL2) for least squares and nanoflann (BSD) for k-d trees. OpenCASCADE (LGPL 2.1 with exception, dynamic link) only when whole freeform bodies are needed.
- Code to adapt from CAMForAlibre `core\`: `Nurbs` (evaluation, derivatives, closest point), `SurfaceGeometry` (analytic sphere, cylinder, cone and torus), `ArcFit` (lines and arcs fitted to polylines), `Boundary`, STL writing from `Simulation`, and `WriteFileAtomically`.

## What the 27.0.1 API offers

From `AlibreX_64.tlh` and `AlibreAddOn_64.tlh`, all unproven:

- **Showing the scan:** add-on graphics in `On3DRender`, as in CAMForAlibre. A 200,000-point polyline took 47 ms there, so large scans need level of detail. Alibre can also hold a mesh natively (`ImportNonNative3DFile`, `GetDesignMeshes`, `AddMeshBooleanFeature`).
- **Picking on the scan:** Alibre doesn't pick add-on graphics, so we cast our own ray from a view command's click with `ScreenToWorld` and the view transform.
- **Creating features:** sketches (lines, arcs, circles, ellipses, B-splines, constraints, dimensions), 3D sketches with B-splines, design planes, axes and points, extrude, revolve, sweep, loft with guide curves, holes, fillets, chamfers, draft, shell, and named parameters.
- **Surfaces:** `IADPartSession::DesignSurfaces->InsertFromFile(file, faceProcessing, heal, makeTolerant, units)` inserts surfaces from STEP, IGES or SAT into an existing part, optionally stitched. Extrudes, cutouts and holes can end `AD_TO_GEOMETRY` at a chosen object.
- **Not available:** surface features (patch, knit, thicken, replace face), and pattern and mirror features.

## The ribbon is the workflow (Frank's decision, 11 November 2026)

The ScanToAlibre ribbon tab's buttons are the steps, in order. They carry no numbers (Frank's decision, 14 November 2026): Show Scan, Load, Refine, Orient, Section, Section Series, Surface, Silhouette, Compare, About.

1. **Load:** reads a scan into the part: STL, and PLY (meshes, and point clouds with normals, which are made into a mesh when loaded; Frank chose that over drawing points as small discs). The mesh is made by a signed distance from the points' tangent planes (Hoppe et al. 1992, Gaussian weighted) on a grid as fine as the points' spacing (coarser for very large clouds), drawn through by surface nets; the surface reaches across gaps between a scanner's lines of points but not past the scan's edges or into holes, so areas the scanner didn't see stay open (Frank is fine with the holes). A cloud without normals is refused for now. The scan is saved inside the part, in the `.AD_PRT`, through Alibre's add-on data stream (Frank chose that over a file beside the part). Loading into a part that has a scan asks before replacing it.
2. **Refine** (Frank asked for a better name than Fix): a modeless dialog that changes the scan straight away, with Undo (up to five changes back).
   - **Decimate:** fewer triangles, to a number or a percent of now, optionally stopping before the surface moves more than a set distance, and optionally keeping open edges in place. Quadric edge collapse (Garland and Heckbert).
   - **Smooth:** a choice of Laplacian (simple; shrinks the shape), Taubin (keeps the size) and Bilateral (bilateral normal filtering; keeps sharp edges, the default for machined parts), with passes, a strength from 0 to 1, and keeping open edges in place.
   - Opening Refine also starts the scan view, so the changes show.
3. **Orient:** a modeless dialog (all Frank's choices, 11 November 2026):
   - **Move** by X, Y and Z (mm), or centre the scan on the origin; **Rotate** about X, Y or Z by an angle, through the origin or the scan's centre; **Scale** by a uniform factor, through either.
   - A **box fitted to the scan**: the smallest box round it, with one axis along a candidate direction (the largest flat areas' normals, the world axes, the principal directions) and turned about it to fit tightest, but square to the part's flat faces and sides wherever that costs at most 5% more (the principal directions alone tilted a lopsided part's box 2 degrees off its flat base), drawn with its edges, three centre planes (A square to the longest direction, then B, C; red, green, blue), three axes and its centre. It's fitted when the scan is loaded or refined, and then moves with it.
   - **Fit Plane** and **Fit Axis:** with **Select Area** down, clicks on the scan grow a selection over a flat area (within an angle and a distance of the area's fitted plane); with **Select Cylinder** down, over a cylinder, outside or a hole (first over the smooth surface, then, once it turns enough to fit, only over triangles on that cylinder). Clicking a selected area takes it away. Fit Plane adds Plane 1, Plane 2 and so on; Fit Axis adds the cylinder's centre line, Axis 1 (diameter, hole or outside) and so on (Frank's request, 14 November 2026).
   - **Align in steps.** Two push-down buttons say what clicks choose: with **Scan** down, a click chooses one of the scan's planes, axes (the box's or fitted) or its centre; with **To** down, a click on one of Alibre's planes, axes or points makes it the target. Pushing one puts the other up, pushing the one that's down puts both up. A plane goes onto a plane, an axis onto an axis, an axis onto a plane (square to it or lying in it, a choice in the dialog; Frank's choice), or a point onto a point. Each step turns and slides only in the ways earlier steps left free, then fixes what it lined up; the first step turns freely, a plane or axis then leaves turning about one line, and a later turn fixes that. A turn that can't also slide onto the target turns only (a side turned parallel to ZX once a hole holds the position), a point slides as near as it can, and a step that lines up nothing new is refused. The dialog says what's still free. Flip turns the scan's plane or axis the other way round. Move, Rotate and Scale start the alignment over.
   - **Delete** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026), beside the Scan feature in the Align group, removes the chosen fitted plane or axis; it's greyed for the box's planes, axes and centre. A plane or axis an alignment step already used stays aligned, as the step moved the scan. New fitted planes and axes are numbered after the highest left, so a name isn't used twice. **Delete All** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026), beside it, removes every fitted plane and axis (symmetry and mid planes too) in one step, after a prompt warning that they're all about to be removed (Frank's correction after his test, 14 November 2026; No is the default), and Undo brings them back; the box's own planes, axes and centre stay.
   - **Find Symmetry** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026) adds the scan's best plane of symmetry as "Symmetry plane n", an ordinary fitted plane for Align, Section and Delete. Starting planes (the box's centre planes, the principal planes through the centroid, and planes from the fitted planes and axes) are scored by mirroring 20,000 points spread over the surface by area and measuring each to the scan; the best four are refined by mirror ICP and the best of those refined further. Planes are ranked by a trimmed rms (each distance capped at 0.2 mm, unmatched points counted at 0.2 mm): ranking by the share within 0.2 mm alone put planes 2 degrees off first on flat-sided parts. A plane with under 30% of the scan within 0.2 mm of its mirror image is refused ("No symmetry found"). A plane already fitted is skipped, so pushing Find Symmetry again adds the next best (Claude's choice).
   - **Mid Plane** (a push-down button): with one plane, axis or point chosen (Scan down), push Mid Plane and click a second of the same kind. Two planes give the plane bisecting them (normals flipped to agree; for parallel planes, halfway between); two axes, the plane square to the shortest line joining them through its middle; two points, likewise. Mixed kinds are refused. It's added as "Mid plane n".
   - Undo takes back moves, fitted planes and axes, deletions and Refine's changes alike, up to five. The scan and its fitted planes and axes are saved in the part (Frank's request, 14 November 2026); the fitted box is worked out again, and the alignment's progress lasts until the part is closed.

Only one of the workflow's dialogs is open at a time (Frank's rule): opening one closes another. **Show Scan** (first on the ribbon, with its own image: Frank's request, 14 November 2026) is a toggle that shows or hides the scan and is the one control for it (Frank's report, 14 November 2026: after a dialog had been open, turning it off left the scan showing until a native command such as Measure). With it off, the scan is drawn only while one of the workflow's dialogs is open, and not at all once the user has turned it off. While it's on, its **Scan tab** in the Design Explorer holds a tree (Frank's request, 14 November 2026): a **Scan Geometry** node with the fitted planes, axes and points under it. Clicking one draws it in yellow over the model; right-click gives **Rename** (edited in place; F2 too) and **Delete** (the Delete key too; Orient's Undo brings it back). A plane of symmetry keeps that role when renamed. Then **About**. Later steps (fitting, sections, surfaces) follow as further buttons.

## Building the part (Frank's decisions, 14 November 2026)

- **Alibre makes the solid features.** ScanToAlibre makes sketches and reference geometry from the scan; Frank extrudes, revolves and drills with Alibre's own commands, which stay fully editable. ScanToAlibre may show measured depths, but doesn't make features.
- **A ribbon button for each action**, after Load, Refine and Orient.
- **First: Section**, a modeless step dialog:
  1. Choose the plane: push **Plane** and click an Alibre design plane or flat face, or push **Scan** and click one of the scan's box or fitted planes (which gets an Alibre design plane made for it, as a sketch needs Alibre geometry). An offset (mm) moves the section along the normal (for an Alibre plane or face, a design plane is made at the offset). There's no Flip: which way the sketch faces is Alibre's, and Extrude can go either way. Each pick is one press: choosing a plane lets the button up, and Create Sketch ends picking and clears Alibre's selection, so several sketches can be made without closing the dialog (pick, Create Sketch, pick again; Frank's request).
  2. The scan is sliced by the plane into polylines (closed loops and open runs), drawn over the scan as soon as the plane or offset changes.
  3. Each polyline is fitted with lines and arcs within a tolerance (default 0.05 mm), joined end to end, drawn over the slice with the largest deviation shown.
  4. Design intent, each a check box: lines within 2 degrees of the sketch's horizontal or vertical made so, joins within 3 degrees made tangent, arcs with centres within the tolerance made concentric, and arc and circle sizes rounded (0.1 mm default, 0.01, or off). The rounded size is the drawn arc's (through its ends), not the free fit's: Alibre's radial dimension scales an arc about its centre, so a size off the drawn arc moved Frank's sketch 1.2 mm off the slice (14 November 2026).
  5. **Sketch Options** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026; a group in Section and Silhouette, all ticked by default): **Create Raw Sketch** (the slice or outline itself), **Create Fitted Sketch** (the fitted lines and arcs with their constraints and dimensions; with both ticked the raw one goes in as construction lines, as before, and with Raw alone as ordinary lines), and **Fit arcs** (unticked, lines only: no arcs or circles). With neither sketch ticked, Create Sketch is greyed.
  6. **Create Sketch:** an Alibre sketch on the plane with the fitted lines and arcs (ends joined), the design intent as Alibre constraints and the rounded sizes as dimensions, **and the original slice as reference geometry** (construction lines along it, simplified to 0.01 mm), so the fit can be judged against the scan.
  7. The scan's extent along the plane's normal is shown as a suggested depth for Alibre's Extrude.
- **Section Series** (Frank's decisions, 14 November 2026, for lofting organic shapes such as `mt12`; plan in `docs\PLAN-section-series.md`), its own ribbon button after Section, a modeless step dialog:
  1. **Plane:** Pick Alibre Plane or Pick Scan Plane, as in Section (one-shot picks). Picking a plane makes the list again: **First** and **Last** default to the scan's extent along the plane's normal less 2% of it at each end, and **Count** (default 10) planes evenly between.
  2. **The list** (one row per plane: offset, plane number, loops, and what the fit makes). **Make** fills it evenly from First to Last. Double-click a row or press F2 to edit its offset; offsets can be negative but must go up from row to row, and one that doesn't is refused with a message, the old one staying. **Add** puts a plane halfway between the chosen row and the next (after the last, one gap further); **Delete** removes the chosen rows. Choosing a row draws its slice thicker and its fit in green; every row's slice is drawn thin red, and the guide curves dark green.
  3. **Create Loft** (default on; Frank renamed it from Optimise for Loft, 14 November 2026) follows each closed loop from slice to slice. Slices whose loops all pair with the next slice's (by centroid and area: within the two loops' sizes of each other, and no more than 25 times the area) make a run; a slice whose loop count changes, or a loop with no partner, starts a new run, and the result line names the runs ("slices 1 to 7: 2 loops; slice 8: 3 loops"). Loops smaller than (10 x tolerance) squared, or a 10,000th of the slice's largest, are left out of matching and written plain, as are open polylines (Claude's choices).
     - **Fit Spline:** a closed spline through N points per slice, the same N in every slice of a run, by arc length from a start: the first slice's sharpest corner (a turn over 30 degrees over a short stretch), then each slice's corner nearest the last start by its angle round the centroid, or with no corner the point at that angle, so the seam stays straight. Corners found in every slice are pinned, so a point falls exactly on each. N doubles from 16 until 99% of every slice's points are within the tolerance of the spline (at most 512).
     - **Use lines and Arcs:** Section's fit of every slice, started at a corner common to every slice, with lines and arcs split in two (never moved) until each stretch between common corners has the same number of segments in every slice. With no common corner (Frank's request after `mt12` fell back to splines, 15 November 2026), each slice starts at its point at the last slice's start angle round the centroid (split there if need be), the joins of every slice are gathered by how far round they are (within 2% of the way round, at most one join of a slice each), and each slice is split where it has no join, so all have the same segments in the same places; a slice that's one whole circle becomes two half arcs. On `mt12cover` that's 47 segments a slice. Only if the counts still differ is that run fitted as splines, and the result line says so. Matched sections are written with their ends joined but no design intent or rounded radii, so Alibre's solver can't move them off each other (it collapsed a tiny split arc into a full circle in Frank's test, 15 November 2026).
  4. **Create Guide Curves** (default on, greyed without Create Loft): a spline through each set of matching points across a run's slices: the pinned or common corners, or with none four points evenly spaced along. Each guide is its own 3D sketch, "Scan Series n guide c.g", as Alibre's loft takes guide curves as sketches (Claude's change to the plan, which had one 3D sketch holding them all).
  5. **Fit** (tolerance, horizontal and vertical, tangent, concentric, round radii) and **Sketch Options** (Create Raw Sketch, Create Fitted Sketch, Fit arcs) as in Section. Symmetric isn't offered yet.
  6. **Create Sketches** (the button reads **Create Loft** while Create Loft is ticked; Frank's request, 14 November 2026): a sketch "Scan Series n.k" for each plane that cuts the scan, on a design plane "Scan Series n.k plane" made at its offset (or the Alibre plane itself at offset 0), with its splines or lines and arcs (moved into the sketch's own axes, so horizontal and vertical are the sketch's), and the raw slice per Sketch Options. With Create Loft, then a loft boss "Scan Series n loft r" through each run's sketches in slice order with its guide curves (Global guides, Claude's choice), made again without the guides if Alibre refuses them, and the result line says so; a run of one slice makes no loft. Before making it, neighbouring sections whose outlines don't overlap seen along the series (Frank's test, 15 November 2026: `mt12`'s last section, 8 mm wide and wholly to one side of the 22 mm one before it, made ACIS's "invalid Body") give a warning naming each pair and the smaller section, recommending removing the small section or adding planes between them so the shape changes slowly, and asking whether to make the loft anyway (No by default; Frank's choice of a warning over ending the run there). This replaced Frank's earlier choice of lofting by hand.
- **Surface** (Frank's decisions, 14 November 2026), a modeless step dialog that fits a B-spline surface to areas of the scan and inserts it into the part as an Alibre design surface, to extrude up to with Alibre's own commands:
  1. **Select:** two push-down buttons, **Smooth Area** (a click takes the area round it over which neighbouring triangles bend less than an angle, default 15 degrees) and **Brush** (a click takes the joined triangles within a radius, default 3 mm). Clicks keep adding to the selection; clicking a selected triangle takes that area away. **Clear** empties it.
  2. **Base plane** (radio buttons): **Auto** (the plane fitted to the selection), **XY**, **YZ** or **ZX**. The surface is a height over this plane, so a selection that folds over it (more than 2% of its area facing more than 85 degrees from the normal) is refused.
  3. **Symmetry** (check boxes): **XY**, **YZ** and **ZX**, mirroring the selection across those world planes, so the surface is exactly symmetric. The base plane's own box is greyed, and at most two can be used. With Auto, the base plane is turned to be square to the ticked planes.
     **Detect** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026) scores XY, YZ and ZX as planes of symmetry of the whole scan as it lies (Find Symmetry's mirror score) and ticks those with at least 80% within 0.2 mm of the mirror image, leaving out the base plane and keeping at most the best two; each score is shown, such as "YZ: 94%". Align the scan's symmetry plane to a world plane in Orient first.
  4. **Surface perimeter** (radio buttons): **Use scan projection** trims the surface to the outline of the whole scan (and its mirrors) projected onto the base plane, holes filled (Frank's correction, 14 November 2026: the whole mesh, not the selection; only the selection is fitted, and the surface carries on smoothly past it); **Use selection** (the default) trims it to the outline of the selection projected the same way; **Use scan bounding rectangle** leaves it the untrimmed rectangle round that projection (plus 2%); **Use scan bounding box** (Frank's request, 14 November 2026) is the untrimmed rectangle round the scan's fitted box (Orient's box) projected onto the base plane, and the selection, plus 2%. An **Offset** (mm, default 0) makes any of them bigger by that much every way (the outline is grown by a disc of the offset); the surface carries on smoothly past the scan there.
  5. **Fit:** a **Tolerance** (default 0.05 mm) and **Smoothing** (0 to 1, default 0.2). Control points are added until 99% of the selection is within the tolerance (at most 40 each way); the dialog shows the counts, the 99% figure, the largest and the RMS deviation. The surface is drawn over the scan as a green grid with its perimeter in yellow (the trim outline, or the rectangle's edges), and fitted again whenever the selection or an option changes.
  6. **Create Surface** writes the surface as IGES (entity 128, or 144 trimmed by a 142 boundary) beside the log and inserts it with `DesignSurfaces.InsertFromFile`.
- **Symmetric** design intent (Frank's request, 14 November 2026), a check box in Section and Silhouette, used when a symmetry plane is square to the sketch plane: the first fitted plane of symmetry (made by Find Symmetry or Mid Plane, whatever it has been renamed to), then the world planes Detect found. Where it meets the sketch plane is the symmetry line. The fit is made symmetric before fitting (Frank's test, 14 November 2026: pairing segments fitted to each side alone found nothing, as the fit broke the two sides into different pieces): a loop crossing the line is cut there, its two halves averaged (each point moved halfway to the other side's mirror image, so a scan that isn't quite symmetric is missed by half as much either side; the result line says how far apart the sides are) and fitted, and the fit's mirror image is the other half; where an end segment meets its mirror at the line (a line within 2 degrees of square to it, or an arc that turns no more than 2 degrees there) the two become one segment across it, an arc fitted again to its points and their mirror images; a loop wholly on one side is paired with its mirror loop (centroid within 5 tolerances or 5% of its size, area within 10%) and one fit serves both; other loops are left as fitted. A construction line goes on the symmetry line, and each joint (or circle's centre) and its exact mirror get Alibre's Symmetric constraint about it. The result line says which plane it used and how many pairs.
- **Silhouette** (Frank's decisions, 14 November 2026: its own ribbon button; every separate piece and every through-hole), Section's dialog with the same plane, offset, fit and design-intent choices, but the sketch is the scan's **outline seen along the plane's normal**, which catches tabs and flanges a slice at one height misses:
  1. The scan's triangles are projected onto the plane and drawn on a grid (cells the larger of the tolerance and a 4000th of the extent), closing gaps of a cell.
  2. Empty regions the outside can't reach are through-holes. A **Smallest hole** (mm across, default 1) fills smaller ones (pinholes, scanner gaps); pieces that small are left out as specks (Claude's choice).
  3. Every boundary loop is traced, and its points moved onto the mesh's own outline edges (open edges, and edges whose triangles face differently along the normal) within 1.5 cells, so the outline is exact rather than stepped.
  4. Each loop is fitted with lines and arcs as Section's slices are, with the same constraints and rounding, and the sketch, "Scan Silhouette n", gets the loops (holes as inner loops) and the outline as construction lines. The suggested depth is the scan's extent along the normal.
- **Compare** (Frank's decisions, 14 November 2026: colour bands, statistics, click to probe, no Update button), a modeless step dialog that colours the scan by its signed distance from the part as built so far:
  1. The part's facets are read when the dialog opens (`IADPartSession::GetFacetData`); open it again after changing the part. With no solid it says "Nothing to compare: the part has no solid yet".
  2. Every scan point's distance to the nearest point of the part, positive outside it, searched within the **Range** (default 1 mm); beyond it a point isn't compared (grey, "not modelled").
  3. **Colour bands**, symmetric about green (Claude's reading of the plan's "8 bands", whose table had 7): dark blue, blue and light blue inside the part (from -range to -tolerance in thirds of the range), green within the **Tolerance** (default 0.1 mm) either way, yellow, orange and red outside, grey not compared. A triangle takes the band of the mean of its corners. A legend shows the colours and their edges. The colours are drawn over the part, so what's inside it shows (Claude's choice).
  4. **Statistics:** how many points were compared and what share, the largest distance outside and inside, the RMS, and the share within the tolerance.
  5. **Probe** (a push-down button): a click on the scan shows the distance there, from its triangle's corners, or "not modelled", and marks the point.
  Changing the tolerance, or narrowing the range, only recolours; widening the range searches again.
- Later buttons: Reference Geometry (the scan's fitted planes, axes and points as Alibre design geometry), Hole (from a fitted hole axis).

## Workflow after Orient (the earlier outline)

1. **Fit primitives:** the user clicks a seed point; region growing and a least-squares fit give a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus, shown with a deviation colour map. Snap to design intent: round values, and parallel, perpendicular or coaxial to features already found.
2. **Sections to sketches:** put a plane through the scan, slice it into a polyline, fit lines and arcs (with tangency), constrain, and create a sketch, then extrude or revolve it. This covers most mechanical parts.
3. **Detail features:** holes, fillets and chamfers from fitted cylinders and blends, created after the base body.
4. **Freeform surfaces:**
   - Fit an oversized, untrimmed B-spline patch to a picked region (projection onto a base plane or cylinder, smoothing, parameter correction), with a deviation map and control over the control-point count and smoothing.
   - Write it as IGES (type 128) or STEP (`B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS`) ourselves.
   - Insert it with `DesignSurfaces.InsertFromFile`.
   - Use it with native features: extrude a sketched outline up to it, cut a block down to it, or end holes at it.
   - Lofts and sweeps through fitted B-spline sections and guide curves, for duct- and handle-like shapes.
   - Whole freeform bodies (patches sewn into a solid) need OpenCASCADE and a STEP import. Later.
5. **Compare:** a deviation map of the finished part (its facets) against the scan.

## First steps

1. Add-on skeleton: solution, `addon\`, `core\`, `tests\`, install scripts, log, a ribbon command.
2. Prove in Alibre:
   - drawing a scan of 1 to 2 million triangles at a usable speed;
   - ray picking with `ScreenToWorld`;
   - `DesignSurfaces.InsertFromFile` of a hand-written IGES B-spline patch;
   - an extrude `AD_TO_GEOMETRY` up to that surface;
   - creating a sketch and an extrude from the add-on;
   - what `AddMeshBooleanFeature` does with a design mesh.
3. Core: scan reader, k-d tree, and plane, cylinder, sphere and cone fitting (region growing and RANSAC), tested on synthetic noisy data.
4. Section to sketch to extrude or revolve: the first end-to-end tool.
